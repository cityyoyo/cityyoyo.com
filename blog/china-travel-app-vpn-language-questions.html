Do You Need a VPN for a China Travel App? And Other Real Questions

Published August 14, 2026  ·  CityYoYo Team

Drafted with AI assistance, reviewed and edited by the CityYoYo team.

Most "best apps for China" lists answer the questions nobody asked and skip the ones that actually stop people from booking the trip.

This is about ChinaWalk, a sister app to CityYoYo built specifically for travelers exploring China — same tap-to-hear narration idea, but tuned for the two things that make China different from a trip to Tokyo or Lisbon: the network, and the language.

Do I need a VPN to use a China travel app?

No — ChinaWalk works in China without a VPN.

This is usually the first thing people ask, and for good reason: plenty of apps and services that work fine at home quietly stop working once you land. ChinaWalk has been tested directly, on the ground, on local carriers and wifi, without a VPN. If you hit a problem while traveling, tell the team — but going in, you shouldn't need to set one up just to use the app.

How do I know what I'm looking at if I can't read Chinese?

ChinaWalk translates the narration into the language you've set — automatically, every time.

Tap a place on the map and it generates a spoken narration for that specific spot, then delivers it in whichever language you've picked for narration: English, Korean, Spanish, Portuguese, or French. You're not reading a plaque you can't understand or juggling a separate translation app mid-narration — the translation happens as part of listening, not as an extra step.

Does it only cover famous landmarks?

No — every place is playable, not just the ones on a curated list.

Most China travel content is built around a fixed shortlist: the Forbidden City, the Bund, the usual dozen. ChinaWalk doesn't pre-select anything. A teahouse with no English signage, a hutong that never made it into a guidebook, an alley you turned into by accident — tap it, and it generates a narration the same way it would for a landmark. That matters more in China than almost anywhere else, since so much of what's worth noticing sits outside the handful of sites every guidebook already covers.

Can I even read the map?

Place names get translated too, not just the narration.

China's maps are commonly labeled in Chinese characters only, which is a barrier before you've even tapped anything — you can't choose what to explore if you can't tell what a pin is. ChinaWalk translates place names on the map and in place lists, so the label problem and the "tell me more" problem are both covered, not just one of them.

What's the best China travel app?

Depends what you're trying to do — China trips usually need more than one app, each solving a different problem:

  • Payments: Alipay or WeChat Pay
  • Navigation: Amap (Gaode Maps) — Google Maps has thin coverage in China
  • Booking transport & hotels: Trip.com or DiDi
  • Text/camera translation: Pleco or Youdao
  • Understanding what you're standing in front of: ChinaWalk

That last one is the gap ChinaWalk fills — none of the apps above tell you the story of the alley you just wandered into.
